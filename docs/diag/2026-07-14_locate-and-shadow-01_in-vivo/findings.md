# Locate + LED writes — full behaviour on the S2000 (`v1.0.26b3-raoul.19`)

## TL;DR

In-vivo continuation of [HARD-03 (#24)](https://github.com/raouldekezel/dolphin-robot/issues/24), driven by elad-bar's follow-up on that ticket and the "what about `locate` on a disconnected robot?" arbitration on the same thread. Six timed sub-tests on the same S2000 within a ~22 min window (11:53:56 → 12:16:23 UTC on 2026-07-14). Four new facts (headline first, hedging where the evidence hedges):

- **Locate is functionally useless on this hardware.** The S2000's LED circuit is only powered while the robot is actually running (`pwsState=on`). In `holdWeekly` — the steady-state of every sleeping robot — LED writes have no physical effect. Raoul confirmed visually across five independent triggers on the dormant robot. The nominal use case ("find the robot at the bottom of the pool") is unreachable by design.
- **SHADOW-01 (new) — `desired → reported` auto-mirror when the robot is dormant but `isConnected=true`.** ~200 ms after the delta the shadow's `desired` is cleared (`{"desired":null}`), then ~2 s later a `reported.led` patch appears that matches what was requested. The physical LED stays off throughout. Reproduced three times in-session. **Author is a hypothesis, not proven:** Raoul's Occam-razor arbitration ([comment](https://github.com/raouldekezel/dolphin-robot/pull/150)) leans PWS — the effect fires under `isConnected=true` and doesn't under `isConnected=false`, so the entity best positioned to draw that line is the PWS itself. The alternative (a cloud-side lambda gated on `isConnected`) is not excluded by anything in the log; a discriminating capture is owed (see the follow-up section).
- **The mirror is confined to `isConnected=true`.** Cas 1bis (real `desired.led=false` delta with `isConnected=false`) sat untouched for 89 s until reconnect. Zero clear, zero mirror. This is the discriminant that made SHADOW-01 nameable.
- **Scenario B confirmed for LED writes (analog of the BUG-21 gate).** The `desired.led=false` parked at T3 was consumed at wake — `reported.led.ledEnable` flipped to `false` at 12:11:00 UTC (~105 s after T3), coinciding with `LastReceiveData.timestamp` advancing to `1784031059` (12:10:59). Consumption at wake is unambiguous; whether by firmware execution or cloud auto-mirror on the rising edge is empirically indistinguishable from this session (the physical LED was off before and after — no visible discriminator).

Two independent confirmations (context, not headline):

- **HARD-03 mutation reproduces on `ledEnable`.** The [2026-07-13 session](../2026-07-13_hard-03_led-eager-write-in-vivo/findings.md) established the shared-reference bug at `aws_client.py:738` on `ledIntensity`; this session confirms the same code path with a different key (three `Set led enabled mode` publishes at T0/T2/T3, all producing a `desired.led` payload that includes the full section from `self.data`). The one-line fix (`dict(...)` shallow copy) closes it for all three LED entities.
- **`is_locating=True` is a dead write.** Three persist events, one per `_vacuum_locate` call in the session (11:53:56.321, 12:00:01.996, 12:06:00.589). No reader for the flag anywhere. Confirms the suspicion in the [#24 arbitration comment](https://github.com/raouldekezel/dolphin-robot/issues/24#issuecomment-…): dead write. A refused Locate under the future gate must not persist it either.

Arbitration for #24:

- **Extend the BUG-21 (#112) Fix-1 gate to `_vacuum_locate`.** Same tri-state read of `SystemDetails.pws_connected`, same `ServiceValidationError` with `translation_key=power_supply_disconnected`, same wording. Ordering constraint: precede the `is_locating` persist so a refused Locate leaves zero side effect.
- **Do not add a `LastReceiveData.timestamp` freshness check to the pessimistic redesign.** This session invalidates it (F4): the field stayed frozen at `1784031059` for the entire active cycle at the end of the session, during which the PWS was demonstrably transmitting (`rTurnOnCount` bumped 116→117 at v2817, `pwsState` moved `holdWeekly`→`on`). The predicate would have rejected T6 — the one write of the day that visibly executed. A better discriminant needs to be worked out inside SHADOW-01.
- **File SHADOW-01 as its own ticket.** The mechanism is nameable ("`reported` moves without `LastReceiveData` and without physical execution, only under `isConnected=true`"); the author is not (see hypothesis above). The ticket carries both the discriminating capture and the counter-observation from v2822 (see F4).

## Findings

### F1 — HARD-03 shared-reference mutation reproduces on `ledEnable` (from mechanism, not from timing)

Three `Set led enabled mode` publishes in this session (T0=11:53:56.322, T2=12:06:00.589, T3=12:09:16.978) all produce a `desired.led` payload that includes the full section (`ledEnable` + `ledIntensity` + `ledMode`) drawn from `self.data`. That's the `_get_led_settings` code path invoked by `_set_led_enabled` — same code, same bug as the [2026-07-13 session](../2026-07-13_hard-03_led-eager-write-in-vivo/findings.md) which established the shared-reference mutation at `aws_client.py:738` on `ledIntensity`. The one-line fix (`dict(self.data.get(DATA_SECTION_LED, default_data))`) is unchanged and closes it for all three LED entities.

Millisecond REST-response timings that were in the earlier draft of this document have been removed — the log filter (`mydolphin_plus|locate|led|isConnected`) does not carry the REST framework's timing lines, and the 2026-07-13 session already pinned the T + 2 ms figure with a proper capture.

### F2 — SHADOW-01: `desired → reported` auto-mirror when the robot is dormant but `isConnected=true`

**Phenomenon (proven):** after any `update/accepted` that emits a `/delta` under `isConnected=true`, the shadow shows two additional writes on the wire, neither originating from the integration:

- **~200 ms after the delta**: `shadow/update/accepted` with payload `{"state":{"desired":null},"metadata":{"desired":{"timestamp":<t>}},"version":<n+1>}`. No `clientToken`. No matching `Publishing` line.
- **~2 s after the delta**: `shadow/update/accepted` with payload `{"state":{"reported":{"led":<requested>}, ...},"metadata":{"reported":{"led":{"ledEnable":{"timestamp":<t+few>}, ...}}},"version":<n+2>}`. Same — cloud-side or PWS-side; not our integration.

Reproduced three times in-session:

- Cas 2bis T0: v2791 (accepted+delta, 11:59:57.121) → v2792 (`desired:null`, 11:59:57.176, **+55 ms after v2791**) → v2793 (reported patch, 11:59:59.064, **+1.94 s after v2792**).
- Cas 2bis T1: v2794 (11:59:57 was Cas 2bis T0; T1 accepted+delta is at 12:00:02.073) → v2795 (`desired:null`, 12:00:02.264, **+191 ms**) → v2796 (reported patch, 12:00:03.733, **+1.47 s**).
- Cas 1ter T5: v2809 (12:12:13.640) → v2810 (`desired:null`, 12:12:13.719, **+79 ms**) → v2811 (reported patch, 12:12:14.144, **+425 ms**).

**Confinement (proven):** Cas 1bis at T3 (12:09:16.966, `isConnected=false`, real delta) is followed only by periodic `get/accepted` at v2799 for the next 89 s — no `desired:null`, no reported patch — until reconnect resets everything. The mechanism is gated by `isConnected=true`.

**Author of the mirror (hypothesis, not proven):** the fake never fires when the PWS is unplugged, and fires reliably when the PWS is plugged in and the shadow reports it as connected. Raoul's Occam-razor arbitration in the PR comment thread: the entity best positioned to draw exactly that line is the PWS itself, so the PWS is the leading suspect. The alternative — a cloud-side lambda that keys off `isConnected` — is equally consistent with all observations to date and cannot be excluded from a shadow-only trace (publisher identity is invisible on our side of the broker). A discriminating experiment is owed: replay Cas 2bis while capturing the PWS's WLAN egress (tcpdump on the router, filtered on the PWS's MAC/IP). Zero outbound packets during the +200 ms / +2 s windows → excludes PWS. An outbound burst correlated with the mirror writes → excludes cloud. Either resolves SHADOW-01's attribution definitively.

### F3 — LED is only physically driven while `pwsState=on`

Raoul's observations, five independent triggers on the sleeping robot (**Cas 2, Cas 2bis T0, Cas 2bis T1, Cas 1ter T4, Cas 1ter T5**): LED off. One trigger on the active robot (Tstart+~30 s, cycle start): LED blinking (matches `ledMode=1`). One trigger during the same active cycle (T6, `ledMode: 1 → 2`): LED switches to continuous on within seconds.

This is a hardware property of the S2000, not a firmware bug: the LED circuit shares power with the pump/motor block, which is unpowered in `holdWeekly`. Consequences:

- Every LED write while `pwsState != on` is a semantic no-op regardless of whether it appears "successful" in the shadow.
- The MyDolphin+ app's precondition for LED controls ("powered on and cleaning") is a UX consequence, not an arbitrary constraint.
- Locate on a dormant robot cannot succeed by construction. The button appears to work in HA (HARD-03 optimistic mutation + SHADOW-01 mirror + `is_locating=True` persist); nothing happens physically.

### F4 — `LastReceiveData.timestamp` is a wake / topic-negotiation marker, not a lie-detector

Two observations across the session:

- **Confirmed wake marker:** the field advanced exactly once, from `1784008812` (06:00:12 UTC — that morning's cycle end) to `1784031059` (12:10:59 UTC), at plug-on during Cas 1ter, coinciding with v2800–v2807 (the wake catch-up trio).
- **Does NOT track live PWS transmissions:** through the entire active cycle at the end of the session (Tstart=12:15:06 to the last printed `get/accepted` at 12:20:41 UTC), `LastReceiveData.timestamp` stayed frozen at `1784031059`. Yet the PWS was demonstrably transmitting: `rTurnOnCount` bumped 116 → **117** at v2817 (12:16:15), `pwsState` transitioned `holdWeekly`→`on`, `robotState` moved `notConnected`→`init`→`scanning`, and v2820 wrote the T6 `ledMode=2` change with fresh metadata timestamps.

The freshness-check design consequence proposed in the earlier draft of this document — "verify `LastReceiveData.timestamp` has advanced since the requested change" — would therefore have **rejected T6, the one write of the day that visibly executed**. Withdrawn.

**Extra counter-observation, worth carrying into SHADOW-01's scope:** v2822 at 12:16:52 UTC — a shadow snapshot that carries fresh metadata timestamps (`1784031412`) and is consistent with a genuine PWS-side transmission (rTurnOnCount and pwsState were established in the same window) — reports `reported.led.ledMode=1`, i.e. the **old** mode, ~29 s after the physical switch to mode 2 was executed. So even authentic-looking PWS reports can be stale by tens of seconds in the LED-section. Any "trust reported" rule needs a discriminant beyond metadata timestamps.

**Alternative discriminant worth testing (not asserted here):** in the three observed SHADOW-01 mirror payloads, `reported` carried the `led` section alone (plus `isConnected` in Cas 2bis's mirrors); authentic multi-section snapshots (like v2789 baseline or v2822) carry `LastReceiveData` + `systemState` + `debug` + many other siblings. So "single-section `reported` patch = suspected mirror" is a promising rule. But this session's evidence is three data points on the mirror side, all under LED writes only — the rule has to be validated on other write paths (LED intensity, LED mode alone, weekly settings, delay settings) before being wired anywhere. Belongs in SHADOW-01, not here.

### F5 — `is_locating` is a dead write (three per-session persists, not four)

`Storing config data, keys: [..., 'locating', ...]` fires exactly at:

- 11:53:56.321 (T0 — Cas 2's Locate)
- 12:00:01.996 (T1 — Cas 2bis's Locate)
- 12:06:00.589 (T2 — Cas 1's Locate hors ligne)

Three entries in `full.mqtt.log`, matching the three `_vacuum_locate` calls in the session. No occurrence at T5 (12:12:13, `light.turn_on`), which enters via `_set_led_enabled` and never passes through `_vacuum_locate` — that block has no Storing line, matching the code path. No reader for the flag anywhere in the log. Confirms the suspicion in the [#24 arbitration comment](https://github.com/raouldekezel/dolphin-robot/issues/24#issuecomment-…): dead write. The write is unconditional under today's ordering (`_vacuum_locate` writes it before publishing). When the BUG-21 (#112) gate extends to Locate (see Consequences), the D2 ordering constraint applies here as well: refuse before persist so a refused Locate leaves no persisted state.

### F6 — Scenario B confirmed for LED writes (Cas 1ter)

`desired.led=false` published at T3=12:09:16 (`isConnected=false`, real delta) sat parked in the shadow — no mirror, no consumption — until reconnect. At TON=12:10:46 the plug came back. At 12:11:00 (**~105 s after T3**, `LastReceiveData.timestamp` jumped to `1784031059` = 12:10:59 in the same window), v2803 posted `reported.led.ledEnable=false` with metadata `1784031060` (12:11:00). The parked `desired` was **consumed at wake** — this is the same class of surprise as BUG-21 Scenario B on the mode-pick path.

**Attribution of the consumption remains empirically ambiguous in this session:**

- **Firmware executed on boot** — the wake sequence includes a shadow sync, and the metadata timestamp is +1 s after `LastReceiveData` advanced, which is consistent with the PWS applying the delta and echoing.
- **Cloud auto-mirror on rising edge** — SHADOW-01 fires on any `update/accepted` under `isConnected=true`; the reconnect could have retriggered it.

The physical LED was off before and after (holdWeekly the whole time), so no visible discriminator. Both interpretations imply the same behavioural consequence: a `desired.led.*` write issued while the PWS is offline is not benign — it will be reflected in the shadow at reconnect. On the S2000, F3 makes the physical execution moot outside an active cycle — but the observable state still lies at reconnect. Same class as BUG-21 Scenario B, one severity level down.

## Consequences

### For BUG-21 (#112) — extend Fix 1 to Locate

Same `pws_connected is False` tri-state check that landed for `_vacuum_start` and `_pickup` in [#149](https://github.com/raouldekezel/dolphin-robot/pull/149). Same `ServiceValidationError` with `translation_key=power_supply_disconnected` (the constant `TRANSLATION_KEY_EXCEPTION_POWER_SUPPLY_DISCONNECTED` in `consts.py` already exists at N=2). Same wording. **Ordering constraint** derived from F5: the check must precede the `is_locating` persist, so a refused Locate leaves no persisted state — mirrors the D2 constraints on the two existing gate sites.

At N=3 consumers of the gate, the helper-extraction nit deferred at #149 is worth doing (`_assert_pws_reachable(action_name)` — the review's rationale for inlining at N=2 was "load-bearing ordering visible at each site"; N=3 is the point).

### For HARD-03 (#24) — the pessimistic redesign needs a different discriminant, not the one this doc originally proposed

Independent of everything else: **the one-line fix at `aws_client.py:738` is still the right thing to do** — it eliminates the HA-side lie regardless of what the cloud or PWS does downstream.

The freshness rule proposed in this document's first draft — "verify `LastReceiveData.timestamp` has advanced since the requested change" — is **withdrawn**. F4 documents both why (the field stays frozen during genuine live cycles) and a candidate alternative (single-section `reported` patch as a mirror-suspect signal) which needs SHADOW-01's own instrumentation to validate before being wired.

**Consider defaulting the FEAT-06 (#143) Locate-hide toggle on for the S2000.** F3 makes it a UX-trap argument (the LED can never be lit outside an active cycle), not a taste call.

### New ticket — SHADOW-01

Proposed title: **"Shadow's `reported.led.*` moves without firmware execution when the robot is dormant but `isConnected=true`"**.

Proposed scope:

- Document the mechanism and its confinement (only under `isConnected=true`; triggered by any `update/accepted` that carries a delta; two-step `desired:null` then single-section `reported` patch shape).
- **Attribution is open** — leading hypothesis (Occam via Raoul's PR arbitration): the PWS itself. Alternative: an AWS/Maytronics cloud-side lambda gated on `isConnected`. The discriminating experiment is a tcpdump on the PWS's WLAN egress during a Cas 2bis replay: zero outbound packets in the +200 ms / +2 s windows excludes the PWS; an outbound burst excludes the cloud.
- Carry the v2822 counter-observation: authentic-looking PWS reports (fresh metadata timestamps, consistent with a genuine snapshot event) can be stale by tens of seconds in the LED-section, at least in the transition from `holdWeekly` to a running cycle. This is a second distinct concern in the same family — "PWS lazy LED reporting" — that may merge with SHADOW-01 if the tcpdump indicts the PWS as the author of the mirror as well.
- Frame the design lever for the pessimistic redesign: a predicate that can distinguish "mirror" from "authentic PWS snapshot". Single-section-vs-multi-section is a candidate; other candidates should emerge from the discriminating capture.
